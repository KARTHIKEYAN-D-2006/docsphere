ABSTRACT
--- P83 [Normal] ---
The project “CampusLife – KRCT: A Unified Hub for Student Resources and Community” presents a centralized mobile application designed to simplify academic resource sharing, campus communication, and daily student activities at K. Ramakrishnan College of Technology (KRCT). The application integrates study materials, campus event updates, a student marketplace, and lost-and-found services into a single platform. It is developed using React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS to provide a user-friendly interface. Supabase manages backend services and authentication, while PostgreSQL stores student details and application data. In-app messaging enables communication between students regarding marketplace items and lost-and-found posts. The admin dashboard helps manage student details, events, listings, and reports. College register number-based registration supports a college-focused community. An AI-powered chatbot helps students find relevant information about study resources, campus events, marketplace listings, and lost-and-found posts. Capacitor supports mobile deployment. Overall, CampusLife aims to improve student communication and provide convenient access to essential college services through a single digital platform.
--- P84 [Normal] ---
Keywords: CampusLife, Student Resources, Mobile Application, Campus Events, Student Marketplace, Lost and Found, AI Chatbot, React, Supabase, PostgreSQL, Capacitor.

TITLE: An Android Application for Campus Information System AUTHOR: Reetu Malhotra, Deepak Kumar, and Dev Priya Gupta (2020)
--- P208 [Body Text] ---
The research paper titled “An Android Application for Campus Information System” focuses on developing a mobile application to improve information sharing and communication within an educational institution. Traditional methods of sharing campus information often depend on notice boards, printed announcements, and separate communication platforms. These methods can make it difficult for students to receive timely updates about academic activities and campus events. The study highlights how mobile applications can provide a convenient way to access important information through smartphones. A campus information system helps bring different types of educational information together in one place. Students can use such applications to stay updated about college activities and access relevant information without depending entirely on manual communication. A centralized platform can also improve the availability of information and reduce the difficulties involved in searching through multiple sources. Mobile-based systems provide a user-friendly approach to campus information management and support communication among members of an educational institution.
--- P209 [Body Text] ---
This study is relevant to the CampusLife-KRCT project because both focus on making campus information easily accessible to students through a digital platform. CampusLife-KRCT extends this concept by combining study material sharing, campus event updates, student marketplace activities, and lost-and-found services in a single mobile application. It also includes in-app messaging to support communication between students and an AI-powered chatbot to help them find relevant information. In addition, the admin dashboard allows administrators to manage student activities and review reported content. Thus, CampusLife-KRCT applies the concept of centralized campus information access to a wider range of student needs.

TITLE: Methods and Frequency of Sharing of Learning Resources by Medical Students
--- P213 [Normal] ---
AUTHOR: Terry Judd and Kristine Elliott (2017)
--- P214 [Body Text] ---

--- P215 [Body Text] ---
The research paper titled “Methods and Frequency of Sharing of Learning Resources by Medical Students” examines how students share academic materials with their classmates and the different technologies they use for this purpose. The study was conducted to understand how frequently students exchange learning resources, what types of materials they share, and which platforms support these activities. The researchers collected responses from 338 students in the first three years of a four-year postgraduate medical curriculum. The study highlights the importance of resource sharing in students’ learning activities. The findings indicate that students shared learning resources with their classmates two or more times per week on average. The resources included study notes, textbooks, curriculum-related materials, and other learning content. Students used different methods to share these resources, including email, social media platforms, and cloud-based file-sharing services. The study also found that students frequently shared materials that were not available through their university's learning management system.
--- P216 [Body Text] ---
This shows that students often depend on peer communication and external digital platforms to obtain useful academic resources.This study is relevant to the CampusLife-KRCT project because one of its main features is the sharing of study materials through a centralized mobile application. Instead of relying on separate messaging applications or other platforms, students can use CampusLife-KRCT to access academic resources in one place. The application also combines study material sharing with campus event updates, marketplace activities, and lost-and-found services. By bringing these features together, CampusLife-KRCT aims to make student information and resources easier to access and share within the college community.

TITLE: An Effective Lost and Found System in University Campus AUTHOR: Siok Yee Tan and Cia Rui Chong – 2023
--- P226 [Body Text] ---
The research paper titled “An Effective Lost and Found System in University Campus” focuses on improving the process of finding lost belongings within a university. Students frequently lose personal items such as identity cards, books, wallets, and electronic devices on campus. Traditional methods of reporting lost items, including informing college staff or sharing information through notice boards, may not help students find their belongings quickly. To address this problem, the researchers proposed a web-based and mobile-based lost-and-found system for the National University of Malaysia (UKM). The main objective was to help students, staff, and visitors report and search for lost items more efficiently.
--- P227 [Body Text] ---
The proposed system provides a login facility and a lost-and-found listing page where users can submit information about lost belongings or items they have found. By maintaining these details in a centralized platform, users can search for relevant information instead of depending entirely on separate communication channels. This approach makes the reporting process more organized and improves the accessibility of information about lost and found items within the university campus.
--- P228 [Body Text] ---
When details about lost items are scattered across different platforms, users may find it difficult to identify matching reports and contact the people who found their belongings. However, a lost-and-found system alone does not cover other important student needs, such as academic resource sharing, campus event updates, and buying or selling items.
--- P229 [Body Text] ---
CampusLife–KRCT: A Unified Hub for Student Resources and Community incorporates a similar lost-and-found facility while extending its services to other campus activities. Students can post information about lost or found belongings and communicate through in-app messaging. Furthermore, an admin dashboard allows administrators to manage lost-and-found records and review reports submitted by students. An AI-powered chatbot helps users find relevant information within the application. Thus, CampusLife-KRCT combines lost-and-found management with multiple student services in a single platform.

--- P233 [Body Text] ---

The study examines how artificial intelligence chatbots can support students and educators in educational institutions. Students often need help finding learning resources, understanding academic topics, and obtaining answers to their questions. Traditional methods may require students to search through multiple websites or wait for assistance from teachers. To address these challenges, the researchers reviewed existing studies to understand the benefits, challenges, and limitations of using AI chatbots in education.
--- P234 [Body Text] ---
The researchers reviewed 67 relevant studies to investigate the role of AI chatbots in educational settings. The findings indicate that chatbots can provide immediate responses to students' questions, explain concepts, and suggest additional learning resources. These capabilities make chatbots useful for improving information accessibility and providing students with convenient support. However, the effectiveness of a chatbot depends on the quality of its responses, the information available to it, and how appropriately it is implemented.
--- P235 [Body Text] ---
The study also discusses challenges associated with AI chatbots, including the possibility of incorrect responses, limitations in understanding users' questions, and concerns about the reliability of the information provided. Therefore, chatbots should be designed carefully and used as supporting tools rather than being treated as completely reliable sources of information.
--- P236 [Body Text] ---
